Reels

Megha, in her own words

Eight short scripts to say to camera. Tap one to open it.

The approach

Simple, spoken, to camera, like the coach reels we studied. Megha talks the way she would over coffee. Six reels are about coaching, told from her own experience: she was coached herself, so she knows its power, and she has spent 25 years in corporate rooms. Two are personal, to introduce her warmly.

How to use the scripts

  • Read it once, then say it in your own words. Nothing to learn by heart.
  • Highlighted [X] parts are yours to fill: a name, a place, a memory.
  • One take is fine. Small pauses make it real.

Getting to know her

01 · Hello, I’m MeghaPersonal · About 1:15

Hi, I’m Megha. I’m a leadership coach here in Dubai, and I’d love to tell you a little about where I come from.

My biggest inspiration has always been my father. [X: what he did, in one line]. Watching him, I learned early what it means to take responsibility for people.

I started my career in [X: first job or city], because [X: what you wanted back then]. Fourteen years in banking followed, at Citibank and Barclays. Then thirteen years building Traveazy, from ten people to more than three hundred.

Along the way I became a mum to two girls, travelled to forty-five countries, and found yoga and meditation.

And now I coach. Because the part I loved most in all those years was never the numbers. It was the people.

I’m really glad you’re here.

Filming

Seated at home, warm light, a slow smile at the end. Keep it unhurried.

Caption

Hello, I’m Megha. A little about where I come from.

02 · What my father taught mePersonal · About 1:00

My father was an entrepreneur. [X: his business, in a few words].

I watched his journey up close. [X: one memory, a hard time or a proud moment].

But what stayed with me most was how he looked at life in his last years. [X: something he said, or how he changed].

It changed how I think about success. [X: what success means to you now, in one line].

A lot of who I am as a coach comes from him. I don’t just ask people what they want to achieve. I ask how they want to live.

For my father, who taught me the most important question.

Coaching, from her own experience

03 · Why I’m a coachCoaching, from her story · About 45 seconds

I’m a coach because I know what coaching can do. I’ve been on the other side of it.

At one point I was [X: where you were in life or work, and when], and a coach asked me a question nobody had asked me in years. [X: the question, if you remember it].

I didn’t have an answer straight away. But when it came, it was mine.

No advice. Nobody telling me what to do. Just space, and the right question at the right time.

That’s when I understood the power of it. And I wanted to give that to other people.

Filming

Straight to camera, one take. Natural light.

Caption

I didn’t start as a coach. I started as someone who needed one.

04 · What I saw in corporate roomsCoaching, from her leadership · About 1:00

For twenty-five years, I sat in corporate rooms. Banking first, at Citibank and Barclays. Then building Traveazy, from ten people to more than three hundred.

And here’s what I noticed. The people who struggled most weren’t the ones without skills. They were the capable ones. Promoted quickly, trusted with a lot, and quietly carrying it all alone.

Leaders get targets. Feedback. Training. What they rarely get is a space that’s entirely about them. Where nobody needs anything from them for an hour.

That’s what coaching is. And it’s the gap I want to fill.

Filming

In her office or at a desk, a little more formal. One cutaway of a city view if easy.

Caption

The capable ones carry the most, and get asked the least.

05 · The moment I live forCoaching, what it feels like · About 35 seconds

My favourite part of coaching is a moment. It happens in almost every session.

Someone is talking, and then they stop. You can see it on their face. They’ve just seen something they hadn’t seen before.

Nobody told them. They got there themselves.

After twenty-five years in leadership, I can honestly say this is the most meaningful work I’ve done.

Filming

Close framing. Let her pause before “Nobody told them.”

Caption

The moment I live for, in almost every session.

06 · What coaching taught me about leadingCoaching, from her leadership · About 30 seconds

What’s really powerful about coaching is that it changes how you listen.

When I trained as a coach, the first thing that changed was [X: one change in how you lead or listen].

I stopped rushing to fix things. I started asking better questions.

And people started telling me what was really going on, not just the polished version.

Filming

Walking slowly, or seated outdoors. Simple.

Caption

Coaching skills are people skills. They changed how I lead.

07 · Signs a coach might helpCoaching, text and voice · 20 to 25 seconds

You don’t need to be in crisis to work with a coach. A few signs it might help:

You’re doing well on paper, but something feels off.

You make decisions for everyone, and nobody asks what you want.

You’re about to change something big. A role, a country, a chapter.

If that sounds familiar, let’s talk.

Filming

Her voice over clean text cards in our colours, one sign per card. Or her to camera with the signs on screen.

Caption

You don’t need a crisis to deserve a coach.

08 · Myth and realityCoaching, text and voice · 15 to 20 seconds

People think coaching is for when something’s wrong.

In my experience, the people who get the most from it are doing well.

They just want to do it on their own terms.

Filming

Two text cards, “Myth” and “Reality”, in sage and mocha, with her voice over. Or one quick take to camera.

Caption

Coaching isn’t a rescue. It’s a choice.
