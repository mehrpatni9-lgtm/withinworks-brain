Megha BhallaExecutive and Transformational Coach · Founder, WithinWorks Leadership1d · 🌐︎···
Last week I sat in a room at the Ritz-Carlton DIFC as Jupiter Wealth looked ahead to 2027: what changes, and what endures. One slide took me straight back to my banking years at Citibank and Barclays. It showed what happens to long-term returns when an investor misses just a handful of the market’s best days. Its title said it plainly: discipline is better than market timing. The lesson I carried out of banking was never really about markets. It was about people. The clients who did well were rarely the ones with the sharpest predictions. They were the ones who knew what mattered to them and stayed with it when the noise got loud. Careers work the same way. We try to time the perfect move: the right moment to leave, to ask, to begin again. Often the real return comes from staying steady on what we value. What changes for you in 2027, and what will you make sure endures? Thank you, @Jupiter Wealth, for a thoughtful afternoon.

07 · Thu 14 Jan

Megha BhallaExecutive and Transformational Coach · Founder, WithinWorks Leadership1d · 🌐︎···
A tool I have come back to this season is the Worry Tree, from Gillian Butler and Tony Hope’s Manage Your Mind. It asks two questions. What am I worried about? Can I do something useful about it now? If yes, decide the next step and take it. If no, park it, and return to today. It sounds almost too simple. That is its strength. Most of us carry worries we cannot act on, as if carrying them were a form of responsibility. It is not. It is only weight. What would you park today?

Why do capable leaders make poor decisions under pressure? Dr Dan Siegel’s window of tolerance offers one answer. When we are pushed too far, we either race or shut down. 14 · Thu 4 Mar

Megha BhallaExecutive and Transformational Coach · Founder, WithinWorks Leadership1d · 🌐︎···
In a session, I may ask a question that surprises people: “Where do you feel it?” It comes from my Co-Active training at CTI. Our bodies often know before our minds are ready to say it. A tight chest before a difficult conversation. A heaviness that arrives every Sunday evening. When we slow down and notice, without rushing to make sense of it, something shifts. The feeling has room to move, and the thinking that follows is clearer. This is a small part of what coaching with me looks like. We stay in the present, we go slowly, and we allow the silence to do some of the work. Next time a decision feels heavy, try asking yourself: where do I feel it?

15 · Thu 11 Mar

Megha BhallaExecutive and Transformational Coach · Founder, WithinWorks Leadership1d · 🌐︎···
A small practice I use before a difficult conversation: Take a deep breath. Take one more. Then release slowly through the mouth. That is all. It takes less than a minute, and it changes the person who walks into the room. Most of us prepare our arguments carefully and arrive with a racing body. The words are ready; we are not. Before your next hard meeting, try preparing yourself as carefully as your slides.
