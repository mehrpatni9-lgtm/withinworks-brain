01 · Mon 30 Nov

Megha BhallaExecutive and Transformational Coach · Founder, WithinWorks Leadership1d · 🌐︎···
Last week I sat in a room at the Ritz-Carlton DIFC as @Jupiter Wealth and its Global CIO, @Diego Wuergler, looked ahead to 2027: what changes, and what endures. One slide took me straight back to my banking years at Citibank and Barclays. It showed what happens to long-term returns when an investor misses just a handful of the market’s best days. Its title said it plainly: discipline is better than market timing. The lesson I carried out of banking was never really about markets. It was about people. The clients who did well were rarely the ones with the sharpest predictions. They were the ones who knew what mattered to them and stayed with it when the noise got loud. Careers work the same way. We try to time the perfect move: the right moment to leave, to ask, to begin again. Often the real return comes from staying steady on what we value. What changes for you in 2027, and what will you make sure endures? Thank you, @Jupiter Wealth, for a thoughtful afternoon at @The Ritz-Carlton, Dubai International Financial Centre.

15 · Wed 6 Jan

Megha BhallaExecutive and Transformational Coach · Founder, WithinWorks Leadership1d · 🌐︎···
In a session, I may ask a question that surprises people: “Where do you feel it?” It comes from my Co-Active training at @Co-Active Training Institute (CTI). Our bodies often know before our minds are ready to say it. A tight chest before a difficult conversation. A heaviness that arrives every Sunday evening. When we slow down and notice, without rushing to make sense of it, something shifts. The feeling has room to move, and the thinking that follows is clearer. This is a small part of what coaching with me looks like. We stay in the present, we go slowly, and we allow the silence to do some of the work. Next time a decision feels heavy, try asking yourself: where do I feel it?

Megha BhallaExecutive and Transformational Coach · Founder, WithinWorks Leadership1d · 🌐︎···
The Bhagavad Gita is one of the oldest leadership conversations I know. On a battlefield, Arjuna, a capable warrior, is frozen by doubt. Krishna does not fight the battle for him. He listens, he helps Arjuna see clearly, and he reminds him who he is. Near the very end, Krishna says, in essence: reflect on all of this fully, and then do as you choose (18.63). As a coach, I find that line remarkable. Clarity first; the choice belongs to the person making it. Another verse I return to often: whatever a leader does, others follow, and the standards they set become the standards of everyone around them (3.21). In the Gita, leadership begins with conduct, long before it reaches strategy. [X: a line on what the Gita means to you and Geet, if you would like to share it.]
