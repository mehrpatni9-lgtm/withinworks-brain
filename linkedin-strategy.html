LinkedIn

Strategy for LinkedIn

How Megha shows up on LinkedIn: her profile, the page, what to post and where to be seen.

Where it stands today

  • 1,692 followers and 500+ connections: a senior Gulf network of bankers, founders and HR leaders.
  • The WithinWorks banner is already up: “The work that matters happens within.”
  • Her own posts stop about four years ago. Since then, only reposts.
  • No About section and no Featured section showing.

Profile fixes

Headline

Today: “Founder, WithinWorks Leadership LLC | Executive & Leadership Coach | CPCC | Former Chief People Officer”.

It calls her an Executive & Leadership Coach, while the website, posts and print all say Executive and Transformational Coach. One title everywhere builds recognition.

Proposed: Executive and Transformational Coach | Founder, WithinWorks Leadership | ICF ACC, CPCC | Former Chief People Officer

For Megha to confirm: whether ACC is through yet, and whether to keep “Former Chief People Officer”. It is a strong credential with HR buyers.

About

Missing today. Three short paragraphs in her voice: the turn from banking and building a company to coaching; who she works with; how a session feels. End on “Reach out” and the website.

Featured

Missing today. Pin three things: the website, the “A New Chapter” document and one Within tool post, such as the Within Compass.

Banner and photo

Both already on brand. Keep them.

Her profile and the page

Voice: I

Megha’s profile

First person, her own story and opinions. This is where trust is built, because people follow people. She reshares every page post with one line of her own.

Voice: we

WithinWorks Leadership page

The business speaking: research, how coaching works, who it is for. Megha in the third person, tagged when it is her insight.

Four pillars

What the coach has been up to

Two learning events a week, each a short reflection with her photo. What she heard, and the question it left her with.

The Within tools

The Within Compass, the Within Core and the tools that follow, explained plainly in a short document.

Notes from 23 years

One moment from Citibank, Barclays or the founding years, and what it taught her. A text post in her voice.

Coaching, plainly

What coaching is and is not, with numbers from the ICF and PwC study, for leaders new to it.

The rhythm

Twice a week

  • Monday: a page document, reshared by Megha with her own line.
  • Thursday: a text post from Megha’s profile, usually the event series or a note from her career.
  • Daily, 15 minutes: thoughtful comments on five posts from people in her network.

Formats that work

Our read of what lands with a senior network

  • Text posts in her voice, 120 to 200 words, opening on a moment rather than a claim.
  • Short documents of three to five pages: one idea, big type, no page numbers.
  • Real event photos with her in them, never stock.
  • Single quote images sparingly. They earn the least on LinkedIn.
  • One quiet line at the end: “Reach out” and the website. Never “book a call”.

Network moves

Where to show up

  • EO: her first room. Share posts in the EO groups and comment on members’ posts.
  • YPO: later, after about six months of steady posting, as she prefers.
  • Ladies Who Lead: tag the network and the photographer in the event post. Join in January if it still fits.
  • BI Women Council: check the fit first, then connect with the organisers on LinkedIn before any email.
  • Comments: a genuine sentence or a question on other leaders’ posts. This builds reach faster than posting more.
  • Events: tag the hosts and speakers in every event post (Jupiter Wealth first).

What a win looks like

By the end of February

  • Profile complete: headline, About and Featured.
  • Twice a week, every week, without gaps.
  • Profile views and follower growth tracked monthly.
  • Messages that start with “I saw your post”: the real measure.
  • The first discovery calls that came through LinkedIn.

Our read: the rhythm, formats and targets are our recommendation, not taken from her profile.
